# Opera selection

Rubric from PLAN.md 4.1: recognition, reducibility, dot-ability, motif-ability, language/tone spread, rights. 1 to 5 each, 30 max. No swaps used — the given sixteen cover the ground.

## The recommended eight

| Title | Language | Tone | Famous aria/theme | Strongest dot idea | Fewest moving parts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carmen | French | Tragedy | Habanera | A dot circles a fixed dot until the circle closes into a blade. | No real leap: a chromatic line creeps down over a static tonic pedal, in the clipped habanera dotted-rhythm; the ache is the pedal refusing to move under it. |
| Pagliacci | Italian | Tragedy (tragic clown) | Vesti la giubba | A dot paints a smile arc over itself while shrinking, then the arc cracks in two. | A falling minor sixth on the cry "Ridi"; a sobbing triplet rhythm; a deceptive cadence lands exactly where the laugh should resolve and doesn't. |
| Rigoletto | Italian | Tragedy | La donna è mobile | A dot changes colour fast while orbiting between two others, then the orbit opens on an empty sack. | A rising fourth to open; a bouncing dotted-triplet dance rhythm; harmony that just oscillates tonic–dominant, never settling — "fickle" as a harmonic fact, not just a lyric. |
| Dido and Aeneas | English | Tragedy | Dido's Lament | One dot sinks straight down a grid column while a fixed descending line ticks beneath it, unchanging. | No melodic leap — the motif is the harmony itself: a descending chromatic fourth ground bass, repeating under a slow stepwise vocal line. |
| The Magic Flute | German | Comedy (fairy tale) | Der Hölle Rache | A dot fractures into a burst of staccato dots that rocket straight off the top of the grid. | An upward leap of an octave or more; a stabbing repeated-note rhythm; a hard diminished jab before the climb, unresolved. |
| Don Giovanni | Italian | Dark comedy | The Commendatore's chords | A grey dot advances one cell at a time toward a red dot that cannot move. | No leap at all — spaced hammer-blow chords are the whole idea; a static minor triad repeated, dread built from stillness, not movement. |
| The Barber of Seville | Italian | Comedy | Largo al factotum | One dot dashes to every corner of the grid in a single breath while the rest freeze. | A rising scalar run that tumbles back down; fast even patter rhythm; a simple tonic–dominant vamp underneath that never varies. |
| Turandot | Italian | Tragedy, turning to dawn | Nessun dorma | A single dot brightens and grows alone as the grid around it fades to black, then white. | A stepwise climb capped by one late, held high leap; rhythm that arrives behind the beat, not on it; a plagal (IV-based) swell into the cadence — acceptance, not victory. |

## The spread

Languages: French (Carmen), English (Dido), German (Magic Flute), and five Italian — Pagliacci, Rigoletto, Don Giovanni, Barber, Turandot. That's Italian-heavy, which is just the repertoire's centre of gravity; the three non-Italian picks each do work nothing else here does (Carmen's chromatic ache, Dido's ground-bass motif, the Queen of the Night's fireworks) rather than being spread for its own sake. Tone: three comedies (Magic Flute, Don Giovanni as dark comedy, Barber) against five tragedies, which clears the "at least two comedies" bar with room, and the comedies sit at opposite temperatures — fairy-tale, dread-under-farce, and pure patter. Tempo and register naturally follow: Barber wants to run fast and high in the mix, Don Giovanni and Dido want to sit low and slow, Turandot and Magic Flute both want to climb, which is a useful contrast to stage rather than a coincidence.

Rights: everything here is pre-1926 and safe everywhere except Turandot, which needs one guardrail — score and evoke only Puccini's own material (Nessun dorma, sung in Act III before Liù's death) and stay well clear of the Alfano-completed ending, which is not Puccini's and carries a different rights story. Nothing else on the list has a caveat.
