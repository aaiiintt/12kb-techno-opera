Eight mini-operas, one card each, per PLAN.md 4.2. Drafts for the brainstorm. Dot ideas follow `operas.md`; none were changed.

### CARMEN (French, tragedy)

- Logline: A soldier falls for a woman who circles him only until the circle closes like a blade.
- Cast: Carmen — red, large (danger and allure), soprano. José — amber, medium, shrinking across the piece (his self-possession), tenor. Chorus is the grid: rings of dots that open like a fan around the pair, then close in as the crowd of the plaza.
- Motif: Evokes the Habanera's chromatic creep over a static tonic — no leap, all descent. Kept: the pedal bass, the dotted habanera rhythm, the sinking line. Scale degrees: `5 4 3 2 1`. Rhythm: `x-x.x-x.`
- Harmony: Root D, Phrygian. A tonic pedal holds under the whole motif; the closing sequence uses planing, the same chord shape sliding down in parallel under the melody.
- Voices and transforms: Carmen (soprano) sings it first, straight. José answers in canon a beat later. Change voice: José takes on Carmen's exact motif on his own tenor timbre when he's absorbed into her orbit. Retrograde plays as he realises he can't undo it. Augment marks the final, unresolved stab.
- Tempo 72, root D, Phrygian, with a ritardando into the final chord.
- Room: bright and wide at first (the plaza), narrowing and darkening as the circle tightens, then near-silent for the stab.
- Sections: Intro (free, ~6s) → Build → Circle → Close → Outro (free, ~6s).
  - STAGE: Carmen enters large and red from the left, orbiting a fixed amber dot at centre. SOUND: motif solo on soprano, room wide open. CUE: L'AMOUR.
  - STAGE: Chorus dots ring both actors loosely. SOUND: arp-as-chorus shimmers the pedal; José answers a beat behind in canon. CUE: TORÉADOR!
  - STAGE: José shrinks slightly, drawn into the orbit. SOUND: change voice — José sings Carmen's motif on tenor; room starts closing. CUE: JE T'AIME.
  - STAGE: The orbit tightens, chorus rings pull inward. SOUND: planing under the motif, feedback climbing, crescendo through the section. CUE: LIBRE.
  - STAGE: Every chorus dot freezes mid-ring. SOUND: one full bar of silence, room muted. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: The orbit snaps shut onto José. SOUND: retrograde, augmented on the last two notes, one dry unresolved chord. CUE: ADIEU.
  - STAGE: José stands alone at centre; the red dot is gone. SOUND: the pedal note holds alone. CUE: CARMEN...
- Turnaround: the lone pedal fades as the chorus drifts back to its opening positions and the room reopens to the plaza setting — the fade-out crowd becomes the fade-in crowd.
- Running time: about 60 seconds.

### PAGLIACCI (Italian, tragedy)

- Logline: A clown paints on a smile that keeps cracking until it splits for good.
- Cast: Canio — amber, large (the public face he performs), tenor. Nedda — blue, medium, always at the edge, out of reach (the private truth he can't get to), soprano. Chorus is the grid: a fixed ring of painted-on seats around the border that never moves, indifferent to what happens inside it.
- Motif: Evokes "Vesti la giubba" — a falling minor sixth on "Ridi," a sobbing rhythm. Kept: the drop, and a sob back up that never quite arrives. Scale degrees: `1 -3 -4 2 1`. Rhythm: `x-x.xx-.`
- Harmony: Root A minor. A deceptive cadence (V lands on vi) hits where the laugh should resolve; a plagal iv-i closes Nedda's private moments.
- Voices and transforms: Canio (tenor) states it first, painting the smile. Fragment: only the first two notes play when he performs for the chorus. Change voice: Nedda answers the same shape brighter, major-flavoured; Canio's tenor answers back minor, re-harmonised. Diminish speeds the motif up as the mask cracks. The final statement is augmented, slow, funereal, as it splits.
- Tempo 96, root A minor. Eases 15% into every deceptive cadence, then snaps back.
- Room: dry and close at first (a single spotlight). Narrows and darkens as the mask strains — muffled even while shouting. Opens fully wet at the crack: the horror finally gets space.
- Sections: Intro (free, ~5s) → Mask → Crack → Split → Outro (free, ~6s).
  - STAGE: Canio appears large at centre, chorus fixed round the edge like painted seats. SOUND: motif solo, tenor, dry room. CUE: RIDI!
  - STAGE: Canio performs — only a fragment of him moves, the rest held in a frozen pose. SOUND: fragment, arp-as-chorus laughing tight and bright beneath. CUE: PAGLIACCIO!
  - STAGE: Nedda's blue dot appears small at the edge, out of reach. SOUND: change voice, brighter, major-flavoured. CUE: NEDDA.
  - STAGE: Canio's dot pulses larger, straining. SOUND: deceptive cadence lands wrong on the beat that should be the laugh. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: The dot flickers, shrinking fast, panicked. SOUND: diminish — double speed, feedback climbing, crescendo through the flicker. CUE: AH!
  - STAGE: The amber dot splits into two smaller dots that fly to opposite corners. SOUND: augment — the final phrase, slow and huge, room wide open. CUE: FINITA.
  - STAGE: The two halves drift small at opposite corners; the border chorus stays fixed. SOUND: a single held bass note, room decaying. CUE: LA COMMEDIA.
- Turnaround: the two halves drift back and re-merge into one amber dot where Canio first appeared, the room's decay resolving into the opening beat's dry spotlight — the split becomes the next entrance.
- Running time: about 60 seconds.

### RIGOLETTO (Italian, tragedy)

- Logline: A jester's curse comes true when he opens a sack expecting his enemy and finds his daughter.
- Cast: Rigoletto — grey, medium, shrinking across the piece (his control over events), bass. Gilda — white, small (her innocence, and how little say she gets), soprano. The Duke — gold, large, always in motion (charm as danger), tenor.
- Motif: Evokes "La donna è mobile" — a rising fourth to open, a bouncing dance rhythm, harmony that oscillates tonic–dominant and never settles. Scale degrees: `1 4 3 2 1`. Rhythm: `x.xx.x.x`
- Harmony: Root G. I–V–I–V–I oscillates, unresolved, until a deceptive cadence opens the sack on the wrong body.
- Voices and transforms: The Duke (tenor) sings it first, bright and careless. Change voice: Rigoletto sings the same shape low on bass when he vows the curse will land elsewhere. Invert: Gilda answers with the mirrored shape — her devotion is his carelessness flipped. Retrograde plays under the sack reveal, the tune running backwards as the plan comes undone.
- Tempo 108, root G major, with a snap ritardando right before the sack opens — the one moment the music refuses to bounce.
- Room: bright and dry through the Duke's charm (a court, not a cathedral). Narrows and darkens the instant Rigoletto believes he's won. Blows wide open, cavernous, at the sack.
- Sections: Intro (free, ~5s) → Court → Vow → Sack → Outro (free, ~6s).
  - STAGE: The Duke, large gold, orbits in a loose figure-eight round the grid. SOUND: motif solo, tenor, bright and dry. CUE: LA DONNA.
  - STAGE: Rigoletto, grey, stands still at centre, watching. SOUND: arp-as-chorus bounces the I–V oscillation beneath the tune. CUE: MOBILE!
  - STAGE: Gilda's white dot appears small at the edge; Rigoletto swells briefly, vowing to protect her. SOUND: change voice — low on bass, heavy where the Duke was light. CUE: MALEDIZIONE.
  - STAGE: The Duke's orbit swings toward Gilda's corner instead of away. SOUND: invert — Gilda answers the mirrored shape. One beat of silence as she's drawn in. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: Rigoletto shrinks, dragging a sack-shaped shadow to centre grid. SOUND: crescendo under a held dominant that refuses to resolve. CUE: VENDETTA!
  - STAGE: He opens the sack; the dot inside is white, not gold. SOUND: deceptive cadence lands exactly as it opens; retrograde plays beneath it. CUE: GILDA!
  - STAGE: Rigoletto, tiny now, kneels beside the white dot at centre. SOUND: a single low bass note holds, room huge and dark. CUE: (silence)
- Turnaround: the held bass note decays as the grid dims to black and the Duke's gold dot reappears at the far edge, resuming his figure-eight exactly as the intro began — the curse resets on the next fool.
- Running time: about 60 seconds.

### DIDO AND AENEAS (English, tragedy)

- Logline: A queen sings her grief once, over a bassline that never stops walking downward, and then she's gone.
- Cast: Dido — violet, large at first, shrinking to nothing (her will to live), soprano. Belinda — pale, small, steady but unable to help (comfort that can't reach her), soprano. Chorus is the grid: a fixed ring, a still court that can only watch.
- Motif: Evokes the ground bass of Dido's Lament — no melodic leap; the motif *is* the harmony, a descending line repeating underneath. The ache is its refusal to stop. Scale degrees (ground bass): `1 -7 -6 -5`. Rhythm: `x-x-x-x-`
- Harmony: Root G minor. The ground bass repeats under every chord change above — pedal point as structure, not texture. Moves used: pedal point throughout and delayed resolution (the bass only ever restarts its descent).
- Voices and transforms: the ground bass (bass voice) states the motif first and never stops. Dido (soprano) enters with a slow stepwise line above it. Augment: as she weakens, her line doubles in length against the unmoved bass. Diminish, briefly: Belinda's attempt at comfort speeds a fragment up, urgent, then it's swallowed back into Dido's augmented line. Change voice at the end: the ground bass migrates from bass onto a hollow arp-as-chorus timbre — Dido is gone, the pattern outlives her.
- Tempo 56, root G minor. The one place in the whole project where tempo does not flex — its refusal to bend is the point. The breath happens in the vocal line above, through augmentation, not tempo.
- Room: half-closed from the start (a court already in mourning). Feedback climbs slowly and steadily; no sudden opens. Grief here is a slope.
- Sections: Intro (free, ~8s) → Ground → Remember me → Outro (free, ~8s).
  - STAGE: One column of the grid lights top to bottom in a slow unbroken line — the ground bass made visible. SOUND: the descending line states alone on bass. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: Dido's violet dot appears large at the top, sinking one row at a time with the column beneath her. SOUND: her slow stepwise line enters above the never-stopping bass. CUE: REMEMBER ME.
  - STAGE: Belinda's pale dot flickers at the edge, unable to change Dido's path. SOUND: diminish — her fragment speeds up, urgent, then is swallowed back into the slow line. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: Dido shrinks as she sinks, smaller with every row. SOUND: augment — her line doubles in length against the unmoved bass. Crescendo builds beneath despite the fall. CUE: BUT AH.
  - STAGE: Dido's dot reaches the bottom row and dims to nothing. SOUND: the vocal line stops; only the ground bass remains, feedback near its peak. CUE: FORGET MY FATE.
  - STAGE: The fixed chorus ring dims uniformly, row by row, in Dido's own pattern. SOUND: change voice — the ground bass migrates onto arp-as-chorus, the pattern outliving the singer. CUE: (silence)
- Turnaround: the ground bass, now on arp-as-chorus, loops to its top note as the dimmed rows relight bottom-up to where a new violet dot appears — descent becomes ascent, room reopening to the intro's half-closed state.
- Running time: about 75 seconds — the lament needs its full patient descent; cutting it under 60 would rush the one gesture (the unbroken bass) that makes it work.

### THE MAGIC FLUTE (German, comedy)

- Logline: A queen commands vengeance and a dot rockets off the top of the world to deliver it.
- Cast: The Queen of the Night — indigo, small but explosively growing (fury), soprano. Pamina — pale pink, medium, still (the thing being fought over), soprano. Sarastro — deep blue, large, stationary (order, calm authority), bass.
- Motif: Evokes "Der Hölle Rache" — an octave-plus leap, a stabbing repeated-note rhythm, a hard jab before the climb. Scale degrees: `1 1 1 5+ 6+`. Rhythm: `x.x.x.x-`
- Harmony: Root D minor. A diminished chord jabs three times before resolving onto a bright major chord at the top — same root, opposite weather when Sarastro answers in the parallel major.
- Voices and transforms: The Queen (soprano) sings the full unstable shape first. Fragment: only the stabs play when she's offstage, a threat implied. Transpose: Pamina sings a smaller version a third down, scared. Change voice plus a major-minor flip: Sarastro answers on bass in the parallel major — calm where the Queen was violent.
- Tempo 132 under the Queen, dropping to 84 under Sarastro — tempo itself characterises the two poles, slowing hard into his first entrance like a door opening onto a calmer room.
- Room: tight and bright under the Queen (a shriek with nowhere to go). Opens wide and warms under Sarastro (a temple). The switch is a hard cut, not a ramp: day and night, no dusk.
- Sections: Intro (free, ~6s) → Fury → Between → Temple → Outro (free, ~6s).
  - STAGE: The indigo dot appears small at the edge, pulsing. SOUND: fragment only, tight dry room. CUE: DER HÖLLE.
  - STAGE: The dot grows enormous, filling several cells, then rockets off the top edge. SOUND: full motif, the stabs resolving into the leap, room shrieking. CUE: RACHE!
  - STAGE: Pamina's pale dot shrinks slightly, caught alone mid-grid. SOUND: transpose, a third down, smaller and scared. One beat of silence. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: Sarastro's dot rises steadily from the bottom edge, large and unhurried. SOUND: hard cut to the warm room; change voice plus major flip. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: Sarastro's dot grows to occupy the centre, Pamina drawn gently toward it. SOUND: crescendo, warm and broad, tempo at its slowest. CUE: WEISHEIT!
  - STAGE: The indigo dot flickers once at the top edge, small again, defeated. SOUND: one last distant fragment, swallowed by the warm room's decay. CUE: HELP!
- Turnaround: the warm room's decay fades to the fragment's stab ticking quietly, which sharpens back into the tight dry room and the small pulsing indigo dot of the first beat — fury waiting under the calm.
- Running time: about 60 seconds.

### DON GIOVANNI (Italian, dark comedy)

- Logline: A statue that cannot move keeps coming anyway, one heavy step at a time, until it arrives.
- Cast: Don Giovanni — scarlet, large, careless, shrinking to nothing at the end (his nerve), tenor. The Commendatore — grey, fixed size throughout, unmoving (that's the point), bass. Donna Elvira — pale gold, small, urgent (how little he listens), soprano.
- Motif: Evokes the Commendatore's chords — no leap, spaced hammer-blow chords, dread from stillness, not movement. Scale degrees: `1 1 1 1` (same note, wide spacing). Rhythm: `x---x---`
- Harmony: Root D minor. A static minor triad hammered at wide, even intervals, never moving until it finally resolves down a step. Moves used: pedal point (the hammer never changes function) and delayed resolution (nothing resolves until Giovanni is caught).
- Voices and transforms: The Commendatore (bass) states the motif first, bare and huge. Giovanni (tenor) answers with the same tune fragmented and diminished into a jaunty triplet — his charm, not a second tune. Elvira (soprano) sings it augmented, slowed further, pleading, over his dance. Invert appears once: Giovanni mocks the statue back in its own shape, upside down — his one act of real defiance, right before it costs him everything.
- Tempo 60 for the hammer; Giovanni's material rides the same clock at diminished note density — stillness and panic share one tempo, with a long held pause before the final chord.
- Room: cold and huge from the first beat (a cellar), because the dread doesn't build, it's simply always there. Giovanni's scenes sit brighter and drier by contrast until the statue's room floods in and overwrites them.
- Sections: Intro (free, ~6s) → Approach → Defiance → Arrival → Outro (free, ~8s).
  - STAGE: The grey dot sits fixed at the far edge, unmoving, oversized shadow beneath it. SOUND: the hammered chord, spaced wide, cold huge room. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: Scarlet Giovanni dances a light path across the near rows, ignoring the grey dot. SOUND: diminish — his fragment dances fast over the same slow hammer. CUE: VIVA LA LIBERTÀ!
  - STAGE: Elvira's dot appears small at the edge, pointing at the grey dot, urgent. SOUND: augment — her line, slow and pleading, over Giovanni's dance. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: The grey dot advances exactly one cell toward Giovanni's row. SOUND: the hammer repeats unchanged, one beat closer. Crescendo begins, almost imperceptibly. CUE: CHI SIETE?
  - STAGE: Giovanni turns to face it, growing briefly larger, defiant. SOUND: invert — he answers the statue's shape upside down, once. CUE: RIDI!
  - STAGE: The grey dot reaches him; Giovanni's dot shrinks to nothing on contact. SOUND: the delayed resolution finally lands, one chord down a step, room floods to full cold reverb. CUE: PENTITI!
  - STAGE: Only the grey dot remains, unchanged in size, where Giovanni used to stand. SOUND: silence, then one soft repeat of the hammer, fading. CUE: (silence)
- Turnaround: the last faint hammer beat decays into the first hammer beat of the next loop, the grey dot never having moved — cold room to cold room, as if it was always waiting.
- Running time: about 60 seconds.

### THE BARBER OF SEVILLE (Italian, comedy)

- Logline: A barber promises he can fix everything and proves it by being everywhere at once.
- Cast: Figaro — orange, medium, constant size but always the fastest thing on the grid (confidence), tenor. Rosina — pink, small, hopeful, soprano. Bartolo — brown, large, immovable and slow (the obstacle), bass.
- Motif: Evokes "Largo al factotum" — a scalar run that tumbles back down, fast even patter, a tonic–dominant vamp that never varies. Scale degrees: `1 2 3 5 4 2 1`. Rhythm: `xxxxxxx-`
- Harmony: Root C. A static I–V–I–V vamp, unchanging throughout — the simplicity is the joke. Move used: establishing home and never leaving, deliberately refusing every other move on the list.
- Voices and transforms: Figaro (tenor) states the motif first at full patter speed. Change voice: Bartolo (bass) manages only the fragment — first three notes — slow and augmented, comically behind. Rosina (soprano) sings it transposed up, brightly, whenever she gets a word in. Diminish: Figaro's own restatements get faster each time, patter escalating until it's barely legible.
- Tempo 152, root C, relentless — the one card where tempo doesn't breathe; the "breath" is Bartolo's fragment landing painfully behind the beat.
- Room: dry and bright throughout, a barbershop not a cathedral, on purpose. One brief, silly, cavernous echo drops in when Bartolo finally lands a note in time, then is yanked dry again.
- Sections: Intro (free, ~4s) → Patter → Chase → Chorus → Outro (free, ~5s).
  - STAGE: Figaro's dot appears and dashes to all four corners in one breath, leaving a bright trail. SOUND: full motif at top speed, dry bright room. CUE: FIGARO!
  - STAGE: Bartolo's large dot sits fixed at centre, trying to keep up. SOUND: change voice — his fragment, badly late, augmented against Figaro's speed. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: Rosina's small dot appears at an edge cell, reaching toward Figaro's trail. SOUND: transpose, bright, up high. CUE: LINDORO!
  - STAGE: Figaro weaves between them, faster each pass. SOUND: diminish — the patter accelerates every repeat, crescendo building through sheer velocity. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: All three dots freeze for exactly one beat, mid-chase. SOUND: hard silence, the patter engine cut dead. CUE: ZITTO!
  - STAGE: Bartolo's dot lands one perfect beat in time, against all odds. SOUND: a sudden cavernous echo for one note, then gone. CUE: BRAVO!
  - STAGE: Figaro dashes once more, full circuit, the others scattering out of his way. SOUND: full motif at maximum speed, huge crescendo into the final vamp chord. CUE: FACTOTUM!
- Turnaround: the vamp chord cuts dead on the beat, half a beat of silence, and Figaro's dot is already back at its entrance position when sound resumes — no fade, because he never actually stopped moving.
- Running time: about 55 seconds.

### TURANDOT (Italian, tragedy turning to dawn)

- Logline: A single voice refuses to sleep until dawn proves it right.
- Cast: Calaf — white-gold, small, growing steadily alone through the whole piece (certainty), tenor. Turandot — ice-blue, large, fixed at centre, cold (absolute power, until it isn't), soprano. Liù — grey, small, shrinking to nothing (the cost nobody else pays), soprano, dimmer register than Turandot's. Chorus is the grid: black at the start, fading to white by the end, the whole city holding its breath.
- Motif: Evokes "Nessun dorma" — a stepwise climb capped by one late, held high note, and a plagal swell into the cadence: acceptance, not victory. Scale degrees: `1 2 3 4 5 7+`. Rhythm: `x.x.x.x.x...x---` (five steps, a breath, then the high note lands late and holds).
- Harmony: Root Ab. Chords climb I–ii–IV, then a plagal swell IV–I at the end. Moves: plagal cadence (the ending) and delayed resolution (the high note arriving late each time).
- Voices and transforms: Calaf (tenor) sings it first, alone, unhurried. Liù sings a fragment — just the climb, no leap — quietly, before fading. Augment: as dawn nears, the whole motif doubles in duration. Change voice at the climax: Turandot herself sings Calaf's motif back to him on her own soprano — the cold voice taking the warm tune, the clearest plot-point transform on the card.
- Tempo 66, root Ab major. Breathes hardest of any card here: every phrase eases late into its final note, the rhythm string's late arrival stacked with a ritardando on top.
- Room: almost fully closed and dark at first (the black city at night). Opens gradually and continuously across the whole piece, no jump cuts — the room itself performing the dawn.
- Sections: Intro (free, ~6s) → Night → Climb → Dawn → Outro (free, ~6s).
  - STAGE: The grid is entirely black; Calaf's small dot appears alone at the bottom edge. SOUND: motif solo, tenor, dark closed room. CUE: NESSUN DORMA.
  - STAGE: Liù's dot flickers faintly at one corner, already small. SOUND: fragment — just the climb, quiet, before she dims further. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: Calaf's dot grows steadily as it rises one row at a time. SOUND: the climb repeats, room opening gradually, feedback easing. CUE: DILEGUA.
  - STAGE: Turandot's fixed dot at centre doesn't move at all while everything else changes. SOUND: pedal-like stillness under the rising motif — she is the one unmoving harmonic anchor. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: Every other dot freezes, holding its breath, as Calaf nears the top row. SOUND: hard silence, one full bar, before the leap. CUE: (silence)
  - STAGE: Calaf's dot reaches the top edge and holds, glowing, arriving a beat later than expected. SOUND: augment — the final phrase doubles in length, the high note landing late. Crescendo through the hold. CUE: VINCERÒ!
  - STAGE: Turandot's dot finally moves, one small step toward Calaf, as the grid turns from black to white. SOUND: change voice — Turandot sings Calaf's motif back, plagal swell resolving beneath. CUE: (silence)
- Turnaround: the full-white grid holds a beat, then dims to black as the room's cutoff resets to its near-closed start — dawn becoming the night before the next dawn, the city holding its breath again.
- Running time: about 70 seconds — the climb needs room to actually climb, and the late-arriving high note only reads if the room has had time to open gradually beneath it; compressing to 60 would make "late" feel merely early-cut.
