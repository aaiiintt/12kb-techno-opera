# The toolset

What we can make with dots, sound and captions, grouped the way a treatment asks for them: set, characters, actions, music, emotion. Every entry was proven in Carmen; the act in brackets is where to find it in `operas/carmen.js`. The kit calls are in `docs/KIT.md`.

## Set

- **A crowd.** About half the grid as dim resting lights (`pilot` in the crowd's colour), still, murmuring: tiny notes from random people. (Seville)
- **Crowd blocks.** Fixed blocks of crowd either side of the stage. They never change size; the people shouting flash. How many flash tells you who's winning. (Toréador)
- **The world goes dark.** Release the crowd's pilots; only the characters rest lit. Use it to move inside someone's head. (The flower, Jealousy)
- **A sway.** Every other cell lit on the bass: the place breathing to the music. (L'amour)
- **Colour of the world.** One staging colour for the place (Seville's sand). Characters and story colours read against it.

## Characters

- **One dot each.** A character is a single cell with a resting light in their colour. They glow when they sing; they never grow, except in a power play.
- **Things they hold are one dot too.** Carmen's flower is one red dot beside José. (The flower)
- **Name them when they enter.** A caption with their name beside them the first time they light. (DON JOSÉ, ESCAMILLO !)
- **A line-up.** Rivals in a row with the heroine between them makes a triangle anyone can read. (Toréador)
- **Changing colour is changing heart.** José's blue turns green with jealousy and back to blue when it's over. (Jealousy, The knife)

## Actions

- **A walk.** `hop` a cell per beat; the camera follows in close. Slow it to one step every two beats for dread. (Seville, The knife)
- **The crowd turns to look.** Neighbours brighten and swell as someone passes. (Seville)
- **An entrance with grandeur.** Hush and drum roll; mid as they walk in through a crowd; close as they land on a hit with a shake; a fanfare; their name. (Toréador)
- **A throw.** A spark arcs cell by cell on rising notes and lands as an object. (The flower)
- **Rings from a character.** Their voice spreading, one ring per note. (L'amour)
- **Ink.** A feeling leaves a character in their colour, a group of cells per note, nearest first, until it surrounds someone. (Jealousy)
- **A hit.** Name it in the silence before; the victim flashes white and red on snares, with a shake. (The knife)
- **A curtain.** Colour falls row by row across the whole grid, wiping everyone but the survivor. (The knife)
- **Rising and falling.** One dot rising off the top is a soul set free; petals drifting down are mourning. (Libre)
- **Three shots.** Wide for the world, mid for two people and the space between, close for one feeling. Cut on the beat; hold a close long enough to read.

## Music

- **The famous tune, quoted,** sung by the character it belongs to. Flag anything written from memory until Iain has heard it.
- **Transform the tune to change who owns it.** Upside down, it's José turning her song against her. (Jealousy)
- **Silence** before the biggest moment; three beats is long enough to hurt. (The knife)
- **The room.** Close it down (low cutoff, more feedback) as the story darkens. (Jealousy, Libre)
- **A heartbeat drum** for love and panic; uneven for panic. (The flower, Jealousy)
- **The lament and the last chords.** A slow theme over a held bass, then fortissimo chords that ring out untouched before the loop. (Libre)

## Emotion

- **Feeling first.** Every act starts from what the audience should feel. Every choice serves it.
- **Motion reads against stillness.** If everything moves, nothing does.
- **Start close on whoever feels it,** then let the feeling leave them in their own colour.
- **Contrast lands endings.** Reuse what the audience already knows, slowed or silenced.
- **Time to wallow.** The ending gets the most time.
- **Captions carry what the dots can't,** one style only: a small white-on-black tag beside a dot, as an `[english, original]` pair (English by default; the corner toggle switches to the opera's language). The pictures must still tell the story without them.
- **Emoji as pictograms.** An emoji at the front of a caption is a silent-film gesture: it reads across languages and before the words do. ✂️ for the barber, 💌 for the letter, 🤫 🧐 😇 for the farce, 💤 for the sleeping guard, 🛑 🏃 🪜 for the chase, ❤️ 🎉 for the wedding. Use the same emoji in both halves of the pair; one per caption; none where the word alone lands harder (MERDE !). (Barber, throughout)
- **The wink.** One or two per opera: a deadpan caption at exactly the wrong moment, with a tiny musical shrug, after a beat of nothing. José's MERDE ! and a falling "uh-oh" just after the knife, before the pity. (The knife)
- **Story colours,** at most three, each meaning one thing (green is jealousy, white is the knife).
