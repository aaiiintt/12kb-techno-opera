# Carmen: treatment

**Hero: Carmen**, not José. She's the one we root for: free, dangerous, doomed.

Colours, from how Carmen is staged:
- **Carmen: red.** Her dress and the flower she throws. The one colour everyone knows.
- **José: dragoon blue.** The soldier's uniform, sober and dutiful.
- **Escamillo: gold.** The toreador's *traje de luces*, the suit of lights. He should glitter.
- **Seville: warm sand.** The sun on the square and the bullring's arena.
- **Night: black.** The smugglers' mountains, and the background.
**Tune:** the Habanera, D minor, 72 bpm, over its dotted bass (dum, da-dum dum).
Opening phrase, *L'amour est un oiseau rebelle*: `1+ 7# 7 6# 6 5`, the chromatic slide from D down to A. Answer, *que nul ne peut apprivoiser*: `5 4 5 6 5 4 3`.
About 60 seconds.

**I. Seville.** (6 s) Heat. The whole screen breathes in dim sand on the habanera bass, each downbeat lifting a column. In the middle of the crowd one cell is red.

**II. L'amour.** (16 s) Carmen sings the slide. Every note lights a ring around her, one ring further out per semitone, so the chromatic descent becomes red rings spreading across the grid. The crowd sways: left half on "dum", right half on "da-dum". Cue: L'AMOUR.

**III. The flower.** (8 s) She throws it: one sakura spark arcs across the grid to a blue cell at the edge. José. His cell blooms, and a halo of blue rings starts to beat like a heart.

**IV. Toréador.** (8 s) Escamillo arrives as a gold comet racing round the outside of the stage on the tune, transposed up, sparkling as he goes: the suit of lights. The crowd of sand flashes in stripes. Carmen's rings turn gold where he passes; José's blue halo shrinks. Cue: TORÉADOR!

**V. Jealousy.** (8 s) José sings her tune back to her, inverted and minor. His blue spreads row by row towards her, a wall eating the red. The room closes.

**VI. The knife.** (4 s) One bar of silence, everything dark. One beat of the arena's sand across the whole screen: the bullring outside, the crowd roaring for Escamillo. Every red ring collapses inward to Carmen, and she burns out cell by cell on the tune, slowed and backwards.

**VII. Libre.** (8 s) José alone in blue. Carmen's embers rise up the screen like petals and out through the top edge. The music box plays the slide once more. The embers become the warm sand breathing of Seville, and it begins again.

**The unforgettable idea:** the Habanera's chromatic slide *is* the picture. Each semitone is a ring of red spreading out from her. You see the tune.
