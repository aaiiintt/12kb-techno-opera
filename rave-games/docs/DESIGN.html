Rave Games · design for approval · 9 Oct

Gaz '89: the people, the places, the beats

Twelve very small games about walking somewhere. This is the design pass Iain asked for before any code: who is in it, where it happens, what is watched and what is played, what we carry over from the arcade and what we leave behind. The brief itself is in GAZ-89.md and is not repeated here.

What we keep from the arcade, what we drop

Keep

  • The WATCH / PLAY grammar. Bars in and one typed line when there is nothing to do; no fuse, no card, no icon when the job is only to walk. The brief's "one or two lines of text" is exactly a WATCH beat.
  • Direct input. Arrows on a keyboard; on a phone, hold a finger where you want him to go and he walks toward it. Nothing else, in any level.
  • The pixel pipeline. Sprites as strings with a palette, baked to one texture, integer scaling, the 3x5 font on a black band. The things that fixed "the shader look" and the blurry type.
  • The synth and the tune format. Scale-degree tunes, the verified-source rule, the Dot Opera voices, drums and room. New instruments for the era (below).
  • The gallery gate. Headless runs that walk the level and photograph it; a sheet a person looks at before anything ships; a size gate in the build.
  • Losing is not a punishment. A fail here is Gaz back at the start of the same screen, instantly, with the tune still playing. No lives, no counter.

Drop

  • The beat grid, the fuse, the verb card, the icons. They exist for ten-second microgames with a twist. These games have one verb and it is always walk.
  • Camera shots and cuts. One fixed screen per level. The brief is right that the motorway feels big with four cars on one screen.
  • Free-text verbs and difficulty set by reasoning. Every level ships with a bot that walks it and a hand-played pass by a person before it is called done.
  • Wide shots with a small cast. Gaz is 12x16 at 2x on a 256x144 screen, so he is never a speck. Rooms are sized to him.
  • Agents writing the story. Captions are fixed in this document and copied verbatim. Agents build rooms and timings, not lines.
  • Bravos and result cards. There is no score. The last screen is Deano by the bike sheds.

The cast

Everyone is 12x16, four colours plus transparent, drawn at 2x. The same walk cycle for all of them (two frames, the arcade's cast library trimmed to walk and stand). Gaz never gets a new frame in twelve levels. These are first sketches at the real pixel size, to be corrected on screen.

The look

256x144, one screen. Backgrounds are solid rectangles and 8x8 tiles in a four-colour palette chosen per level; the palette is the time of day. Light is the subject: a landing light under a door, sodium orange, fluorescent white, full beam, torch beams, the pale grey of a Sunday morning. Text is the 3x5 font, always on a black band, never on the picture.

The twelve, beat by beat

Each level: the opening line (typed on the bottom bar while the picture holds, three seconds, then the bars go and he can move), the picture, the one job, what fails it, what ends it, and a watched moment at the end before the next level loads. Time is a target for a player who knows what they are doing; a first play is longer.

  1. 1ORDINARY

    Laindon Bedroom top-down · ~25 s

    Opening
    SATURDAY, 7PM. MUM THINKS HE IS STAYING IN.
    Picture
    His room on the left (bed, a tape deck, posters as coloured rectangles), the landing across the middle, the stairs at the right edge. Mum crosses the landing between the bathroom and her room on a loop, a tea in her hand. Palette: landing-light yellow, brown carpet, two blues.
    Play
    Walk from the bed to the top of the stairs without being on the landing while she is. Her door creaks a beat before she comes out; that is the tell.
    Fail
    She sees him: GAZ?, he is back on the bed. The tune does not stop.
    End
    He reaches the stairs. Watched: he goes down, the front door clicks. HE SHUTS THE DOOR QUIETLY. TOO QUIETLY.
  2. 2THE CALL

    The Phone Box side view · ~20 s

    Opening
    LAINDON HIGH STREET. DEAD BY NINE.
    Picture
    A long dark street, shuttered shops, two sodium lamps, a phone box at the far right with two panes gone. It is ringing: the box flashes faintly with the ring. Palette: black, sodium orange, dark red, grey.
    Play
    Walk right to the box. Nothing stops him. A dog barks somewhere. The ringing gets louder as he gets closer, which is the whole mechanic.
    Fail
    None. If he dawdles the ringing stops for two seconds and starts again.
    End
    Watched: he picks up. DEANO: M25. TONIGHT. BRING MONEY. then HE HAS £11.
  3. 3REFUSAL

    The Kitchen side view · ~30 s

    Opening
    HE SAID HE IS STAYING AT DEANO'S. SHE DOES NOT BELIEVE HIM.
    Picture
    A small kitchen, strip light, a table in the middle, the back door at the right. Mum moves between the sink, the kettle and the table, and when she turns to face him he has to stop (walking while she faces him is "answering back"). Palette: fluorescent white, mint units, brown lino, red.
    Play
    Get round the table to the back door, moving only when her back is turned. Each time she turns, a tag: WHOSE CAR? WHAT TIME? HAVE YOU EATEN?
    Fail
    Moving while she faces him: GARY. He is back by the fridge.
    End
    He reaches the door. Watched: she puts a fiver on the table without looking. SHE KNOWS. SHE GIVES HIM A FIVER ANYWAY. £16.
  4. 4MENTOR

    Behind the Glue Pot side view · ~15 s

    Opening
    BASILDON. ROUND THE BACK. TERRY HAS BEEN TO SHOOM.
    Picture
    A pub's back wall, bins, one security light, Terry leaning on the wall at the far side smoking. Palette: brick red, black, one white light, grey.
    Play
    Walk across the yard to him. Nothing happens. That is the point: the walk is the nerves.
    Fail
    None.
    End
    Watched: Terry points off-screen right, once. TERRY: JUNCTION 28. FOLLOW THE NOVAS. THAT IS ALL HE SAYS.
  5. 5THRESHOLD

    Onto the M25 top-down · ~30 s

    Opening
    DEANO IS PARKED ON THE OTHER SIDE OF THE SLIP ROAD.
    Picture
    Four lanes top to bottom, headlights as white blocks with a red tail, the lay-by at the top with a Nova and Deano beside it flashing the headlights. Palette: tarmac grey, white, red, sodium.
    Play
    Cross. Four cars on screen at most, slow lanes and a fast lane. Frogger with no logs and no timer.
    Fail
    A horn, a white flash, back at the bottom. No death animation; he was never hit, he just jumped back.
    End
    Watched: he gets in the Nova. The headlights swing off. THE NOVA DOES 60 IF YOU ARE PATIENT.
  6. 6TESTS

    Thurrock Services top-down · ~30 s

    Opening
    THURROCK SERVICES, 11PM. EVERYONE HERE IS GOING WHERE HE IS GOING.
    Picture
    A concourse left to right under fluorescent white, a fruit machine, a Wimpy sign, small groups of people in bright tops standing about. One bouncer by the far door who turns on a slow loop. Palette: fluorescent white, lino grey, a fruit-machine pink, black.
    Play
    Get to the far door. Pass the bouncer when his back is turned. The crowd are obstacles that shuffle, never block.
    Fail
    He turns while Gaz is in his cone: NOT TONIGHT, SON. Back to the near door.
    End
    Watched: outside, the car park, every car full. EVERY CAR IN THE CAR PARK IS GOING TO THE SAME FIELD.
  7. 7THE CAVE

    The Ongar Lanes top-down maze · ~40 s

    Opening
    OFF THE MOTORWAY. NO SIGNS. HEDGES.
    Picture
    Black. A small maze of lanes (about 12x7 cells) you only see as the Nova's headlights light them: a cone ahead, nothing else. Every so often a car comes the other way with full beam and the whole screen goes white for a frame. Palette: black, hedge green, headlight yellow, white.
    Play
    The Nova (Gaz is in it; the sprite is the car) drives the lanes with the arrows. Find the exit, which is the only cell with a glow of light beyond it.
    Fail
    None. Dead ends are the fail. A full-beam flash wipes what you had seen.
    End
    Watched: a field gate, a line of cars, blue lights far off. THEY ARE NOT THE ONLY ONES WHO FOUND IT.
  8. 8ORDEAL

    The Police Roadblock top-down · ~35 s

    Opening
    THE POLICE HAVE THE GATE. THE FIELD IS THE OTHER WAY ROUND.
    Picture
    An open field, mud furrows as lines, a hedge with one gap at the top. Three torch beams sweep from the bottom edge, long cones that cross. Palette: mud brown, black, torch white, hedge green.
    Play
    Walk to the gap. Don't be in a beam. The beams are slow; the furrows are not obstacles, just texture.
    Fail
    Lit: OI!, the beam holds, he is back at the bottom. Three fails and the beams get slower, quietly.
    End
    Watched: through the gap, the sound comes up before the picture does. HE CAN HEAR IT NOW.
  9. 9REWARD

    The Field top-down · ~30 s, no fail

    Opening
    None. The music is already playing when the picture comes.
    Picture
    The field at night from above, a rig at the top with a single strobe, a crowd of hundreds as two-pixel figures bobbing, a few lasers as lines. Palette: black, strobe white, acid green, a warm orange from a generator lamp.
    Play
    Walk into the crowd. As he gets closer the music opens up (the room filter lifts, the bass comes in) and the figures around him start bobbing in time with him. There is nothing to get wrong.
    Fail
    None, by design.
    End
    When he is in the middle, the level does not end. It holds for as long as the player wants; the first tap after fifteen seconds fades to white. No caption at all.
  10. 10ROAD BACK

    The A127 Hard Shoulder side view · ~45 s

    Opening
    SUNDAY, 7AM. THE NOVA HAS DIED ON THE A127.
    Picture
    A very long strip (the only level that scrolls, slowly). Pale grey sky, a crash barrier, the Nova with its bonnet up and Deano asleep in it at the left, a phone box a long way right. Palette: pale grey, white, barrier silver, red.
    Play
    Walk right. Slowly: his walk is at two-thirds speed here. A lorry passes every twenty seconds. That is all.
    Fail
    None.
    End
    Watched: he picks up, says nothing we see. TERRY SAYS HE WILL BE AN HOUR. HE HAS £2.
  11. 11RESURRECTION

    47 Denehouse Lane side view · ~20 s

    Opening
    LAINDON, 11AM. THE FRONT DOOR IS ALREADY OPEN.
    Picture
    A row of three identical houses, his in the middle, Mum in the open doorway with her arms folded. Daylight, flat. Palette: pebbledash grey, white, a red door, a blue sky.
    Play
    Walk up the path to the door. As he gets closer the tune slows down, pitch and all, like a tape running out. Nothing stops him.
    Fail
    None.
    End
    Watched: she steps aside. MUM: THERE'S TOAST. THAT IS THE WHOLE CONVERSATION.
  12. 12ELIXIR

    Laindon Comprehensive side view · ~20 s

    Opening
    MONDAY. NOTHING HAS CHANGED.
    Picture
    A concrete playground, a fence, the bike sheds at the right with Deano leaning on them, kids as small moving pairs. Palette: concrete grey, school-jumper maroon, white, sky.
    Play
    Walk across to Deano. The kids drift; none of them look up.
    Fail
    None.
    End
    Watched: they nod. One nod each, two frames. Then black. GAZ '89 and nothing else. No "the end", no credits, no score.

The watched beats, as a rule

Each level has exactly two: the opening line while the picture holds (bars in, typed, three seconds), and the ending (bars in, something small happens, one line, the next level loads on a tap). Nothing in the middle is ever watched. Mum's, Deano's and Terry's words are tags beside them, in the arcade's one caption style. The running total of Gaz's money appears three times (£11, £16, £2) and is the only number in the whole thing.

Music: what carries over

The synth is ready for this. What it needs is three new instruments and a rule about the tunes.

Reuse

  • The Dot Opera synth: scheduling on the audio clock, the room (delay and filter, which is how the field "opens up" in level 9 and how level 11 slows down), the drum kit, the arpeggiator and drones.
  • The tune format in lib/tunes.js: scale degrees and durations, one loop per level, 4 to 8 bars.
  • The verification rule: a tune is only called a quote when it has been checked against a source; otherwise it is "in the key, in the style".

New

  • Three instruments: a 303-style acid line (saw, resonant lowpass with an envelope on the cutoff), a 909-style kit (the existing kick and hat, plus a clap), and a piano stab (three detuned pulses with a fast decay) for the Italo and Detroit levels.
  • Each level's loop is written as an original in the manner of the named record: its tempo, its kit, its chord move, its feel. Not its melody. See question 1.
  • Level 9 is the one long piece (two minutes, looping, no edits), everything else is 20 to 40 seconds looped.

Characters' rules

  • Gaz never speaks on screen. Not once. Everyone else gets at most one tag per level.
  • Mum is never a villain. She is in two levels and the doorway. Her lines are practical.
  • Deano is the only one who looks like he is having a good time. He is asleep in level 10.
  • Terry is in one level and says one thing. He does not come to the rave.
  • Nobody explains acid house. The word "rave" appears in the opening line of level 2 and never again.

Questions for you

  1. The tunes. The brief names twelve records. We cannot quote their melodies note for note: they are in copyright, and the arcade's rule is "verified quote or not a quote". Recommendation: originals in the manner of each record (its tempo, kit, chord move and sound), with the record named only in the source notes. The alternative is a very short motif of each, under two bars, which is still a quote. Which?
  2. Size. "Under 128 KB": per game on disk, uncompressed, including the engine? The engine core is about 38 KB minified, so a level has 90 KB to itself, which is far more than it needs. Recommendation: gate at 128 KB uncompressed per level and report the gzipped size too.
  3. Touch. Arrows on a keyboard is clear. On a phone: hold a finger where you want him to go and he walks toward it (the arcade's drag), or a tap on the screen's edges as a d-pad? Recommendation: hold-to-walk-toward.
  4. Chaining. Twelve pages that each load the next, as the brief says, or one page that holds all twelve and remembers where you got to? Twelve pages is truer to the brief; one page is simpler to share and to play on a phone. Recommendation: one page, twelve levels, with a URL per level so each is still its own thing.
  5. Level 7. The brief has Gaz on foot everywhere except here, where the sprite has to be the Nova. Keep that (the one level where he is not the sprite is the one where he is most lost), or put him on foot in the lanes too?
  6. The money. £11, £16, £2 as the only numbers in the game, three captions. Keep, or drop as too cute?
  7. Level 9's hold. It does not end until the player taps, after fifteen seconds. Right, or should it fade on its own?
  8. Build order, once this is approved. Fable: the shell (one page, levels as data, the walk, the two watched beats, the bot, the gallery, the size gate), the cast sprites, the three instruments, and level 1 as the worked example. Then Sonnet agents, three at a time, one level each, with this document as the only source of lines and palettes; Fable judges every sheet and plays every level by hand before it is called done. New budget needed: the arcade used $66 of its $70. Estimate for all twelve: $45 to $60.
