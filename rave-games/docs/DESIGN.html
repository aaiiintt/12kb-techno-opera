Rave Games · design, revised with Iain's notes · 9 Oct

Gaz '89: the people, the places, the beats

Twelve very small games about walking somewhere. This is the design pass Iain asked for before any code: who is in it, where it happens, what is watched and what is played, what we carry over from the arcade and what we leave behind. The brief itself is in GAZ-89.md and is not repeated here.

What we keep from the arcade, what we drop

Keep

  • The WATCH / PLAY grammar. Bars in and one typed line when there is nothing to do; no fuse, no card, no icon when the job is only to walk. The brief's "one or two lines of text" is exactly a WATCH beat.
  • Direct input. Arrows on a keyboard; on a phone, hold a finger where you want him to go and he walks toward it. Nothing else, in any level.
  • The pixel pipeline. Sprites as strings with a palette, baked to one texture, integer scaling, the 3x5 font on a black band. The things that fixed "the shader look" and the blurry type.
  • The synth and the tune format. Scale-degree tunes, the verified-source rule, the Dot Opera voices, drums and room. New instruments for the era (below).
  • The gallery gate. Headless runs that walk the level and photograph it; a sheet a person looks at before anything ships; a size gate in the build.
  • Losing is not a punishment. A fail here is Gaz back at the start of the same screen, instantly, with the tune still playing. No lives, no counter.

Drop

  • The beat grid, the fuse, the verb card, the icons. They exist for ten-second microgames with a twist. These games have one verb and it is always walk.
  • Camera shots and cuts. One fixed screen per level. The brief is right that the motorway feels big with four cars on one screen.
  • Free-text verbs and difficulty set by reasoning. Every level ships with a bot that walks it and a hand-played pass by a person before it is called done.
  • Wide shots with a small cast. Gaz is 12x16 at 2x on a 256x144 screen, so he is never a speck. Rooms are sized to him.
  • Agents writing the story. Captions are fixed in this document and copied verbatim. Agents build rooms and timings, not lines.
  • Bravos and result cards. There is no score. The last screen is Deano by the bike sheds.

The cast

Everyone is 12x16, four colours plus transparent, drawn at 2x. The same walk cycle for all of them (two frames, the arcade's cast library trimmed to walk and stand). Gaz never gets a new frame in twelve levels. In the two driving levels the sprite is the Nova, seen from above, and Gaz is in it; the walk becomes a steer, left and right only. These are first sketches at the real pixel size, to be corrected on screen.

The look

256x144, one screen. Backgrounds are solid rectangles and 8x8 tiles in a four-colour palette chosen per level; the palette is the time of day. Light is the subject, and engine 1.27's 2D light system does it for real: lamps with a glow, headlight and torch cones with soft edges, a strobe, a sun for the mornings. Each level says what its light is. Light is the subject: a landing light under a door, sodium orange, fluorescent white, full beam, torch beams, the pale grey of a Sunday morning. Text is the 3x5 font, always on a black band, never on the picture.

The twelve, beat by beat

Each level: the opening line (typed on the bottom bar while the picture holds, three seconds, then the bars go and he can move), the picture, the one job, what fails it, what ends it, and a watched moment at the end before the next level loads. Time is a target for a player who knows what they are doing; a first play is longer.

  1. 1ORDINARY

    Laindon Bedroom top-down · ~25 s

    Opening
    SATURDAY, 7PM. MUM THINKS HE IS STAYING IN.
    Picture
    His room on the left (bed, a radio-cassette on the floor, posters as coloured rectangles), the landing across the middle, the stairs at the right edge. Mum crosses the landing between the bathroom and her room on a loop, a tea in her hand. Palette: landing-light yellow, brown carpet, two blues.
    Radio
    The tune is coming out of his radio-cassette, tuned to a pirate station, so it is thin and crackly in the room and gets quieter as he leaves it. Before the picture opens, over black, the station: ...THIS IS CENTREFORCE, 88.3... ...SHOUT GOING OUT TO THE BASILDON POSSE... ...INFO LINE IS 0836... typed like the captions, with static between. The rave is announced before he has left his room.
    Light
    Dark house, one lamp on the landing. Mum's door opening is the light under it getting brighter: that is the tell, a beat before she comes out. The stairwell is the darkest thing on screen.
    Play
    Walk from the bed to the top of the stairs without being on the landing while she is.
    Fail
    GAZ? and he is back on the bed. The radio does not stop.
    End
    He reaches the stairs. Watched: he goes down, the front door clicks. HE SHUTS THE DOOR QUIETLY. TOO QUIETLY.
  2. 2THE CALL

    The Phone Box side view · ~30 s, the long walk

    Opening
    LAINDON HIGH STREET. DEAD BY NINE.
    Picture
    The full width of the screen is the street: shuttered shops, a chippy with its light still on, a bus shelter, two sodium lamps, a phone box at the far right with two panes gone. He starts at the far left. Palette: black, sodium orange, dark red, grey.
    Sound
    Things happen as he passes them, in audio and in tags: a dog yapping behind a fence YAP YAP YAP, a telly through a window, a car going by too fast, a shutter rattling, a cat, the chippy door, someone's mum shouting a name that is not his. The phone starts ringing when he is halfway, and gets louder as he gets closer. That is the whole level.
    Light
    The two lamps with a hazy glow, dark between them, the chippy a warm block, the phone box flashing faintly with each ring.
    Play
    Walk right. Nothing stops him. Slowly.
    Fail
    None. If he dawdles the ringing stops for two seconds and starts again.
    End
    Watched: he picks up. DEANO: M25. TONIGHT. BRING MONEY. then HE HAS £11.
  3. 3REFUSAL

    The Kitchen side view · ~30 s

    Opening
    HE SAID HE IS STAYING AT DEANO'S. SHE DOES NOT BELIEVE HIM.
    Picture
    A small kitchen under a strip light, the table in the middle, the back door at the right. Mum moves between the sink, the kettle and the table. Palette: fluorescent white, mint units, brown lino, red.
    Dialogue
    This level is the dialogue. Every time she turns round she asks something, one tag, and there are a lot of them, in a different order each go: WHOSE CAR? WHAT TIME? HAVE YOU EATEN? IS DEANO'S MUM IN? YOU'VE GOT NO COAT. WHAT'S IN THE BAG? RING ME. GARY. He never answers. The kettle clicks off at some point and she turns to it, which is the longest gap.
    Light
    Flat fluorescent, nothing to hide in. The back door is a dark blue rectangle of outside.
    Play
    Get round the table to the back door, moving only when her back is turned. Simple. He gets as many goes as he needs, and nothing is lost by needing them.
    Fail
    Moving while she faces him: GARY. and he is back by the fridge, and she has a new question.
    End
    He reaches the door. Watched: she puts a fiver on the table without looking. SHE KNOWS. SHE GIVES HIM A FIVER ANYWAY. £16.
  4. 4MENTOR

    Behind the Glue Pot side view · ~25 s, the long walk

    Opening
    BASILDON. ROUND THE BACK. TERRY HAS BEEN TO SHOOM.
    Picture
    The whole width of the screen is the yard: the pub's long back wall, bins, a fire door, a stack of crates, one security light on the wall, Terry at the far right leaning on the wall smoking, the cigarette end the only moving thing. Gaz starts at the far left. Palette: brick red, black, one white light, grey.
    Light
    One security light halfway along; it clicks on as he passes under it and off again behind him. Terry is in the dark except the cigarette.
    Play
    Walk across the yard to him. Nothing happens. The walk is the nerves, and the width of the screen is the walk.
    Fail
    None.
    End
    Watched: Terry points off-screen right, once. TERRY: JUNCTION 28. FOLLOW THE NOVAS. THAT IS ALL HE SAYS.
  5. 5THRESHOLD

    Onto the M25 top-down driving · ~40 s

    Opening
    DEANO'S NOVA. THE M25. IT STILL FEELS ENORMOUS.
    Picture
    The road scrolls down the screen, three lanes, the Nova near the bottom. Other cars pass or get passed, mostly full of people going where they are going, a lorry now and then, the junction signs and the cat's eyes. Spy Hunter with nothing to shoot. Palette: tarmac grey, white, red, sodium.
    Dialogue
    The conversation is the level. Deano and Gaz in tags above the car, and other cars' windows as they pass: DEANO: TERRY SAID 28. DEANO: THAT'S 28. DEANO: THAT WAS 28. A car full of people going past: OI OI! A lorry: nothing. The music is louder than the kitchen was, with the window down.
    Light
    Headlights as cones on the road ahead, tail lights red, the sodium lamps on the overbridges passing overhead in a rhythm, cat's eyes catching the beams.
    Play
    Steer left and right to stay in a lane and not touch anything. Nothing comes at you fast. The road is the long walk, driven.
    Fail
    A bump: a horn, the car shunts back to the middle lane. Low stakes, no restart.
    End
    The junction. Watched: the slip road, the Nova's indicator ticking. THEY COME OFF AT 28. THERE ARE NOVAS EVERYWHERE.
  6. 6TESTS

    Thurrock Services top-down · ~30 s

    Opening
    THURROCK SERVICES, 11PM. EVERYONE HERE IS GOING WHERE HE IS GOING.
    Picture
    A concourse left to right under fluorescent white, a fruit machine, a Wimpy sign, small groups in bright tops standing about. One bouncer by the far door who turns on a slow loop. Palette: fluorescent white, lino grey, a fruit-machine pink, black.
    Dialogue
    The bouncer talks to himself and to everyone, one tag each time he turns: NOT TONIGHT, SON. I KNOW WHERE YOU'RE GOING. I WAS AT SHOOM. MY NEPHEW DJS. GO ON THEN. The groups talk too as Gaz passes them: IS IT NEAR ONGAR? HE'S GOT THE MAP. WE'VE BEEN HERE AN HOUR.
    Light
    Flat fluorescent inside, the fruit machine flashing pink, the far doors a block of sodium orange from the car park.
    Play
    Get to the far door. Pass the bouncer when his back is turned. The crowd shuffles, never blocks.
    Fail
    He turns while Gaz is in his cone: a line from him, Gaz back to the near door.
    End
    Watched: the car park, every car full. EVERY CAR IN THE CAR PARK IS GOING TO THE SAME FIELD.
  7. 7THE CAVE

    The Ongar Lanes top-down driving · ~40 s

    Opening
    OFF THE MOTORWAY. NO SIGNS. FOLLOW THE CAR IN FRONT.
    Picture
    The same driving as level 5, but a single-track lane that winds, hedges either side, and one set of tail lights ahead: the car they are following. It slows at junctions, indicates, goes. Lose it and the lane is black. Palette: black, hedge green, headlight yellow, white.
    Dialogue
    Out of the windows: DEANO: THAT'S NOT TERRY'S MATE. DEANO: IT IS. HE'S GOT A SUNROOF. DEANO: LEFT. LEFT. NO. From the car ahead at a junction: ARE YOU LOT GOING TO IT? FOLLOW US. A car coming the other way with full beam: DEANO: DIP YOUR LIGHTS, MATE.
    Light
    The Nova's two headlight cones are the only light; the hedges appear in them and go. Full beam the other way floods the screen white for a second. The car ahead is two red dots.
    Play
    Steer to keep the tail lights in view through the bends. Low stakes, nostalgic, a bit nervous.
    Fail
    None. Falling behind means a black screen for a moment and the tail lights come back round the next bend.
    End
    Watched: a field gate, a line of cars, blue lights far off. THEY ARE NOT THE ONLY ONES WHO FOUND IT.
  8. 8ORDEAL

    The Police Roadblock top-down · ~35 s

    Opening
    THE POLICE HAVE THE GATE. THE FIELD IS THE OTHER WAY ROUND.
    Picture
    An open field, mud furrows as lines, a hedge with one gap at the top. Three torch beams sweep from the bottom edge, long cones that cross. Palette: mud brown, black, torch white, hedge green.
    Light
    The torches are light cones with soft edges; being lit is the fail check, literally. A blue light pulses behind the hedge at the bottom. The gap at the top has a faint glow from the field beyond.
    Play
    Walk to the gap. Don't be in a beam. The beams are slow and regular, and there is always a dark path if you wait for it.
    Difficulty
    Tuned by hand to be got in two or three goes, not one and not ten. The beams never speed up. After three fails they slow down a little, quietly.
    Fail
    OI!, the beam holds on him, he is back at the bottom. The music does not stop.
    End
    Watched: through the gap, the sound comes up before the picture does. HE CAN HEAR IT NOW.
  9. 9REWARD

    The Field top-down · as long as you like, no fail

    Opening
    None. The music is already playing when the picture comes.
    Picture
    The field at night from above, a rig at the top with a strobe, a crowd of hundreds as two-pixel figures bobbing, a few lasers as lines, a generator lamp. Palette: black, strobe white, acid green, warm orange.
    The toy
    This is a playable toy, the reward in the middle of the thing. Where he stands is the mix: closer to the rig the filter opens, the bass comes in, the strobe is faster and the people round him bob in time with him; further back it is a wash with the top end gone; out to the sides the balance shifts, the left speaker stack or the right. Walk about and the music follows. Nothing to get wrong.
    Light
    The strobe is a light that blinks at the tempo, the lasers thin cones sweeping, the generator lamp warm and still. In the middle of the crowd it is bright; at the edges it is dark and the figures are silhouettes.
    Play
    Walk into the crowd, and about in it.
    Fail
    None, by design.
    End
    It does not end on its own. After fifteen seconds a small TAP TO LEAVE appears in the corner and stays; a tap fades to white. Leave whenever you want; no one is forced to stay. No caption at all.
  10. 10ROAD BACK

    The A127 Hard Shoulder side view · ~45 s

    Opening
    SUNDAY, 7AM. THE NOVA HAS DIED ON THE A127.
    Picture
    A very long strip (the only level that scrolls, slowly). Pale grey sky, a crash barrier, the Nova with its bonnet up and Deano asleep in it at the left, a phone box a long way right. Palette: pale grey, white, barrier silver, red.
    Play
    Walk right. Slowly: his walk is at two-thirds speed here. A lorry passes every twenty seconds. That is all.
    Fail
    None.
    End
    Watched: he picks up, says nothing we see. TERRY SAYS HE WILL BE AN HOUR. HE HAS £2.
  11. 11RESURRECTION

    47 Denehouse Lane side view · ~20 s

    Opening
    LAINDON, 11AM. THE FRONT DOOR IS ALREADY OPEN.
    Picture
    A row of three identical houses, his in the middle, Mum in the open doorway with her arms folded. Daylight, flat. Palette: pebbledash grey, white, a red door, a blue sky.
    Play
    Walk up the path to the door. As he gets closer the tune slows down, pitch and all, like a tape running out. Nothing stops him.
    Fail
    None.
    End
    Watched: she steps aside. MUM: THERE'S TOAST. THAT IS THE WHOLE CONVERSATION.
  12. 12ELIXIR

    Laindon Comprehensive side view · ~20 s

    Opening
    MONDAY. NOTHING HAS CHANGED.
    Picture
    A concrete playground, a fence, the bike sheds at the right with Deano leaning on them, kids as small moving pairs. Palette: concrete grey, school-jumper maroon, white, sky.
    Play
    Walk across to Deano. The kids drift; none of them look up.
    Fail
    None.
    End
    Watched: they nod. One nod each, two frames. Then black. GAZ '89 and nothing else. No "the end", no credits, no score.

The watched beats, as a rule

Each level has exactly two: the opening line while the picture holds (bars in, typed, three seconds), and the ending (bars in, something small happens, one line, the next level loads on a tap). Level 1 also has the radio over black before its picture. Nothing in the middle is ever watched. Dialogue during play is tags beside whoever is talking, never a bar caption, and nothing waits for the player to read it. Mum's, Deano's and Terry's words are tags beside them, in the arcade's one caption style. The running total of Gaz's money appears three times (£11, £16, £2) and is the only number in the whole thing.

Music: what carries over

The synth is ready for this. What it needs is three new instruments and a rule about the tunes.

Reuse

  • The Dot Opera synth: scheduling on the audio clock, the room (delay and filter, which is how the field "opens up" in level 9 and how level 11 slows down), the drum kit, the arpeggiator and drones.
  • The tune format in lib/tunes.js: scale degrees and durations, one loop per level, 4 to 8 bars.
  • The verification rule: a tune is only called a quote when it has been checked against a source; otherwise it is "in the key, in the style".

New

  • Three instruments: a 303-style acid line (saw, resonant lowpass with an envelope on the cutoff), a 909-style kit (the existing kick and hat, plus a clap), and a piano stab (three detuned pulses with a fast decay) for the Italo and Detroit levels.
  • Each level's loop is written as an original in the manner of the named record: its tempo, its kit, its chord move, its feel. Not its melody. See question 1.
  • Level 9 is the one long piece (two minutes, looping, no edits), everything else is 20 to 40 seconds looped.

Characters' rules

  • Gaz never speaks on screen. Not once. Everyone else gets at most one tag per level.
  • Mum is never a villain. She is in two levels and the doorway. Her lines are practical.
  • Deano is the only one who looks like he is having a good time. He is asleep in level 10.
  • Terry is in one level and says one thing. He does not come to the rave.
  • Nobody explains acid house. The word "rave" appears in the opening line of level 2 and never again.

Decisions (Iain, 9 Oct)

  1. Tunes are originals in the manner of each record. Level 1's comes out of a pirate radio with an announcement before the picture.
  2. Size: 128 KB on disk per level page, engine included; one page with all twelve as well, with a URL per level.
  3. Touch: hold a finger where you want him to go. Driving levels: hold left or right of the car.
  4. Levels 5 and 7 are both top-down driving in the Spy Hunter manner, low stakes, about the music and the talk out of the windows. 5 is the motorway at night; 7 is following one car's tail lights down the lanes.
  5. Levels 2 and 4 are long walks across the full width of the screen with things happening in sound.
  6. Level 3 is kept simple and is about the dialogue; unlimited goes.
  7. Level 6 keeps the bouncer and gets more talk.
  8. Level 8's difficulty is tuned by hand: two or three goes.
  9. Level 9 is a toy: where he stands is the mix; it never forces anyone to stay, TAP TO LEAVE after fifteen seconds.
  10. The money captions stay. The light system is used wherever a level names its light.
