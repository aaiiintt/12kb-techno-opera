# 05. The next seven

A starting brief for each remaining opera, in site order. Each is a first pass at steps 1 and 2 of the workflow (acts, what happens, the feeling), plus the tune, the colours, the characters, the toolset test the opera poses, and wink candidates. Treat them as drafts to argue with, not treatments to build. Every tune here is written from memory: mark it `fromMemory: true` and get Iain to listen before you trust it.

Research with the musical analysis of each aria ("fewest moving parts") is in `docs/archive/operas.md`.

## Recommended build order

Iain's site order is Carmen, Pagliacci, Rigoletto, Dido, Flute, Giovanni, Barber, Turandot. For building, do the ones that stress the toolset differently first, so the toolset grows early:

1. **Dido and Aeneas**: stillness, one lament, a ground bass. The opposite of Carmen; the test that the toolset transfers.
2. **Il barbiere di Siviglia**: pure comedy and speed. Tests patter, farce, a chase.
3. **Don Giovanni**: dread through stillness, and the statue. Tests menace.
4. **Die Zauberflöte**: fairy tale and fireworks. Tests a light show and the "purple section" ascension.
5. **Pagliacci**, **Rigoletto**: two Italian tragedies that will reuse a lot of Carmen.
6. **Turandot** last: the biggest ending in the series; by then the lament and dawn techniques exist.

Propose this to Iain; he decides.

## The shape they share

Every opera: 5 to 7 acts, about 60 to 90 s, one to three characters as single dots, a crowd where the story has one, the famous tune sung by the character it belongs to, one story colour or two at most, one wink, and an ending that takes its time (lament or apotheosis, last chords, blackout, silence, then the loop).

## Pagliacci (Leoncavallo, Italian)

**Story in a line.** A clown learns his wife loves another man, has to go on stage and play the cuckold for laughs, and kills her in front of the audience.

| Act | Happens | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SI PUÒ? | Tonio steps in front of the curtain: what you'll see is real. | Curiosity, unease |
| IL VILLAGGIO | The troupe's cart rolls into the village; the crowd gathers. | Holiday |
| NEDDA | Nedda and Silvio, in secret; Tonio watches. | Tenderness, then being watched |
| VESTI LA GIUBBA | Canio alone, putting on the costume, "Ridi, Pagliaccio". | Heartbreak; the mask goes on |
| LA COMMEDIA | The play, with the village laughing; Canio stops acting. | Laughter curdling |
| NO, PAGLIACCIO NON SON | He demands the name; she refuses; the knife; Silvio too. | Horror in public |
| LA COMMEDIA È FINITA | The crowd, silent. | Emptiness |

**Tune.** "Vesti la giubba", the "Ridi, Pagliaccio" cry: a sob on a falling phrase (research notes a falling minor sixth and a sobbing triplet). Sung by Canio (tenor). Consider the play-within-a-play's jaunty minuet as the contrast.

**Colours.** Clown white with red pom-poms (Canio's Pagliaccio costume; white is his greasepaint, the mask: story colour); Nedda in Colombina's pale blue or pink; village sunlight (a warm yellow); Silvio in earth brown. Story colour: the white mask, which goes on in Act IV and is what he wears when he kills.

**Characters.** Canio (tenor, red), Nedda (soprano), Tonio (bass, the watcher), Silvio (a dot that must not be named). The village is the crowd, and the crowd is also the audience of the play.

**The toolset test.** A crowd that laughs (flashing in yellow) at a scene the audience knows isn't funny. Putting a mask on: a character's colour swapping to white while his voice keeps singing in red's feeling. A stage within the stage: a smaller lit rectangle inside the grid.

**Wink candidates.** "LA COMMEDIA È FINITA" is the opera's own last line and is already the deadpan beat; give it to Tonio, flat, after the silence. Or the crowd's "BRAVO!" a beat too late.

**Watch for.** Two stabbings in a row: the second (Silvio) can be a single flash off in the crowd, not a second set piece.

## Rigoletto (Verdi, Italian)

**Story in a line.** A jester mocks a grieving father and is cursed; he hires an assassin to kill the Duke who seduced his daughter, and finds his daughter in the sack.

| Act | Happens | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LA CORTE | The Duke's court, all pleasure; Rigoletto mocks; Monterone's curse. | Glitter, then a chill |
| GILDA | Rigoletto with his hidden daughter; the Duke woos her as a student. | Protective, sweet |
| CARO NOME | Gilda alone with his name; the courtiers carry her off. | Innocence, then theft |
| CORTIGIANI | Rigoletto begs the courtiers; gets her back, ruined. | Humiliation |
| LA DONNA È MOBILE | The inn: the Duke sings; the assassin's sister pleads for him. | Charm you can't trust |
| LA TEMPESTA | Storm; Gilda knocks; the knife meant for the Duke. | Dread |
| LA MALEDIZIONE | The sack; the Duke's tune offstage; Gilda dies in his arms. | The floor gives way |

**Tune.** "La donna è mobile" (the Duke, tenor): rising fourth, bouncing dotted rhythm, tonic and dominant only. Its return offstage in the last act, cheerful, while Rigoletto opens the sack, is the opera's cruelest moment and the picture: the tune's colour flickering far off-grid while the centre goes dark.

**Colours.** The jester's motley (two-tone gold and green, bells); the Duke in crimson; Gilda in white; night and storm in blue-black; the sack (a dim dot that is her).

**Characters.** Rigoletto (bass or tenor, motley), Gilda (soprano, white), the Duke (tenor, crimson). Courtiers as a crowd that mocks; the storm as the grid.

**The toolset test.** A curse: something set at the start that returns at the end (a colour, a chord). Dramatic irony: a caption or tune coming from off-grid while the focus is elsewhere. A storm on the whole grid (the sway technique, violent).

**Wink candidates.** The Duke's tag "LA DONNA È MOBILE" drifting in from the far edge as Rigoletto opens the sack is the real thing; don't add a joke on top. If a wink is wanted, Rigoletto's flat "BENE." when the assassin quotes his price.

## Dido and Aeneas (Purcell, English)

**Story in a line.** The queen of Carthage lets herself love a visiting prince; witches trick him into leaving; she dies of it, singing "Remember me".

| Act | Happens | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SHAKE THE CLOUD | Dido troubled; Belinda coaxes her; Aeneas arrives. | Reluctance melting |
| THE HUNT | A grove, the court at ease, the sky darkens. | Ease, then weather |
| THE WITCHES | The Sorceress plots; a false Mercury commands Aeneas to sail. | Malice enjoying itself |
| COME AWAY | The sailors prepare; Aeneas tells her; she sends him away. | Pride and hurt |
| WHEN I AM LAID | The lament over the ground bass; "Remember me". | Grief, held still |
| WITH DROOPING WINGS | Cupids scatter roses on her tomb. | Quiet |

**Tune.** Dido's Lament, "When I am laid in earth", in G minor over a five-bar ground bass: a descending chromatic line (1, 7#, 7, 6#, 6, 5, then a cadence, roughly 3 4 5 1-), repeated unchanged under a slow stepwise vocal line. Note that Carmen's Habanera slide is the same chromatic descent; the code for it transfers, but the effect must be the opposite of Carmen: the ground bass is fixed, ticking beneath her, unchanging, while she sinks.

**Colours.** Tyrian purple (Dido, royalty of Carthage); Trojan bronze or red (Aeneas); sea and sky blue (the world he sails into); a poisonous green-black (the witches); rose (the cupids' flowers at the end). The witches' green must not read as Carmen's jealousy green: choose a colder tone.

**Characters.** Dido (soprano, purple), Aeneas (tenor, bronze), Belinda (a paler purple beside Dido). The witches as a trio of dots; the sailors as a small crowd; the sea as the grid.

**The toolset test.** Stillness as the whole act: one dot sinking a column while a fixed line ticks below. A ground bass as a visual (the same row lighting the same way every five bars). A departure: a dot leaving the grid by the edge, slowly, while she doesn't move. Roses laid one by one, still, as an ending that isn't petals falling (Carmen has those).

**Wink candidates.** The Sorceress's line is already comic: "HARM'S OUR DELIGHT". Or Aeneas's feeble "I'LL STAY" answered by "AWAY, AWAY!". English captions here are the opera's own language, so the plot is legible for once; keep them sparse anyway.

## Die Zauberflöte (Mozart, German)

**Story in a line.** A prince and a bird-catcher set out to rescue a princess from a sorcerer, discover the sorcerer is good and her mother the Queen of the Night is not, and pass the trials of fire and water.

| Act | Happens | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DIE SCHLANGE | Tamino chased by the serpent; three ladies kill it. | Panic, then rescue |
| DER VOGELFÄNGER | Papageno, feathers and pipes, wants a wife. | Delight |
| DIES BILDNIS | Tamino sees Pamina's picture; the Queen appears; the flute and bells. | Wonder, a mission |
| DER HÖLLE RACHE | The Queen's fury: kill Sarastro. | Fireworks; fear made beautiful |
| DIE PRÜFUNGEN | Silence, fire, water, with the flute. | Ordeal, then calm |
| PA-PA-PA | Papageno finds Papagena. | Pure joy |
| DIE SONNE | The Queen's forces sink; the sun rises. | Triumph, light |

**Tune.** "Der Hölle Rache" (the Queen of the Night, soprano): the staccato high arpeggios, an upward leap, hammering repeated notes. Also Papageno's "Der Vogelfänger bin ich ja" and his bells (the arp voice, literally a glockenspiel), and the pa-pa-pa duet.

**Colours.** Night blue with silver stars (the Queen); sun gold (Sarastro); green feathers (Papageno); Pamina in white; fire orange and water blue for the trials. The serpent: a line of green cells.

**Characters.** Tamino (tenor), Pamina (soprano), Papageno (the comic lead, and the kawaii heart of the piece), the Queen (soprano; a light show more than a dot), Sarastro (bass; the sun).

**The toolset test.** A light show: the Queen's aria as staccato dots rocketing off the top of the grid, one per note. Comedy timing: pa-pa-pa as two dots stammering towards each other. The purple section: the trials as ascending full chords with ring pulses and the room opening (Iain's favourite moment in the original; it belongs here). A serpent: a moving line of cells.

**Wink candidates.** PA-PA-PA-PA-PAGENO / PA-PA-PA-PA-PAGENA is built in. Papageno's padlock (the three ladies lock his mouth for lying): a caption "HM HM HM HM".

**Watch for.** Too many characters. Cut Monostatos and the three boys. Keep the plot to: rescue, the Queen's true face, the trials, the joy.

## Don Giovanni (Mozart, Italian, dramma giocoso)

**Story in a line.** A seducer kills a father, laughs at everyone, invites the dead man's statue to dinner, and is dragged to hell for refusing to repent.

| Act | Happens | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IL COMMENDATORE | Night; Giovanni flees Anna; the father duels and dies. | Shock in the dark |
| IL CATALOGO | Leporello's list: 640, 231, 100, 91, and in Spain 1,003. | Appalled laughter |
| LÀ CI DAREM | Giovanni charms Zerlina; Elvira interrupts. | Seduction, gently |
| LA FESTA | The party; masks; everyone against him; he laughs. | Glitter and menace |
| IL CIMITERO | The graveyard; the statue speaks; the invitation. | The hair stands up |
| A CENAR TECO | Dinner; the statue's steps; "Pentiti!" "No!"; hell. | Dread, then flames |
| QUESTO È IL FIN | The others, in daylight, sing the moral. | Relief, a shrug |

**Tune.** The Commendatore's chords (D minor, the overture's opening hammer blows: a static minor chord repeated, dread built from stillness), plus "Là ci darem la mano" (major, lilting) as its opposite. The two together are the opera.

**Colours.** Stone grey (the statue: story colour, the only grey in the series); Giovanni in black with a red lining (cape); Leporello in a dull brown; Anna and Elvira in mourning violet; Zerlina in a peasant green; hellfire orange.

**Characters.** Giovanni (bass or tenor), Leporello (bass), the Commendatore (bass; first a dot, then a grey dot that moves one cell at a time). Women as three dots he passes between. The party as a crowd.

**The toolset test.** Menace by stillness: a grey dot advancing one cell per chord, never faster, while Giovanni doesn't move. A catalogue: the crowd as a count, cells lighting in blocks of a hundred with a caption of the number. Hell: the grid burning from the bottom up (fire as a curtain rising, the reverse of Carmen's). A moral ending: the remaining dots in a neat row, bright, as if nothing happened.

**Wink candidates.** "MILLE E TRE" as the crowd count lands. Giovanni's "NO!" to the statue, then, dragged under, a tiny "AHI." Leporello hiding under the table: a dot ducking a row.

## Il barbiere di Siviglia (Rossini, Italian)

**Story in a line.** A count wants a girl locked up by her guardian; the town's barber engineers disguises, bribes and a storm until she's his.

| Act | Happens | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ECCO RIDENTE | Dawn serenade under Rosina's window. | Hopeful, silly |
| LARGO AL FACTOTUM | Figaro: everyone wants him, everywhere at once. | Energy, delight |
| UNA VOCE POCO FA | Rosina: sweet, and she'll get her way. | Mischief |
| LA CALUNNIA | Basilio: slander starts as a breeze and ends as a cannon shot. | Gleeful malice |
| IL SOLDATO | The Count as a drunk soldier; chaos in Bartolo's house. | Farce |
| LA TEMPESTA | The storm; the ladder; the notary; Bartolo outwitted. | Hurry, hurry |
| FELICITÀ | Wedding. | Happy ending |

**Tune.** "Largo al factotum" (Figaro, baritone: sing it with the tenor voice, an octave down if needed): the "Figaro qua, Figaro là, Figaro su, Figaro giù" patter, a rising scalar run tumbling back down, a tonic-dominant vamp.

**Colours.** Seville again: sand (the same light as Carmen's, a series in-joke), but at dawn and night: a moonlit blue, a gold window. Figaro in a bright barber's blue-and-white stripe (pick one: white); Rosina in rose (sakura); Bartolo in a fusty brown; the Count changes colour with each disguise.

**Characters.** Figaro (the hero dot; everywhere), Rosina (in a window: a lit cell inside a dark block), the Count (a dot that changes disguise: colour), Bartolo (a dot that blocks doors).

**The toolset test.** Speed: one dot dashing to every corner of the grid in a single breath while everyone else freezes. Disguise: a character changing colour and the others not noticing (a caption "?"). Slander: a whisper spreading cell by cell into the whole grid shaking. A window: a lit cell inside a dark block; a ladder: a column of cells lighting upward.

**Wink candidates.** "FIGARO! FIGARO! FIGARO!" from three different dots at once. Bartolo's "?" as the disguised Count walks past him again.

**Status.** A draft exists (`operas/barber.js`, `docs/treatments/barber.json`, parallel session, 27 Sep) with a cast roll-call opening, Figaro as a Nokia snake through the crowd, a sleeping guard, a lightning ladder and a pink wedding wave, and emoji-led bilingual captions throughout. Review it act by act with Iain before building on it.

**Watch for.** Comedy needs the same feeling-first discipline: every act still has one feeling. Patter is rhythm, so the arp voice can carry the words.

## Turandot (Puccini, Italian)

**Story in a line.** A prince answers the ice princess's three riddles and wins her, but offers her a way out: guess his name by dawn. The city may not sleep. A slave girl dies rather than tell it.

| Act | Happens | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LA LUNA | Peking; the decree; a suitor executed as the moon rises. | Cold ceremony |
| IL GONG | Calaf sees Turandot and strikes the gong. | Reckless awe |
| GLI ENIGMI | Three riddles: hope, blood, Turandot. He answers all three. | Tension, three times |
| IL NOME | His own riddle: his name by dawn. | A gamble |
| NESSUN DORMA | Nobody sleeps; the city searches; "Vincerò". | Yearning that becomes certainty |
| LIÙ | Liù tortured, keeps the name, takes the knife herself. | Pity |
| L'ALBA | Dawn. | Light, at last |

**Tune.** "Nessun dorma" (Calaf, tenor): a stepwise climb capped by one late held high note on "Vincerò", arriving behind the beat, a plagal swell into the cadence (acceptance, not victory). This is the series' biggest sung moment; build the whole opera towards it.

**Rights guardrail.** Puccini died before finishing the opera; the ending was completed by Alfano and has a different rights story. Score and stage only Puccini's own material: everything up to and including Liù's death. End on Liù, or on dawn as light with no music that isn't Puccini's.

**Colours.** Ice white and silver-blue (Turandot); imperial red and gold (Peking, the court); the moon (a pale dot rising); Liù in a soft pink; the gong in bronze.

**Characters.** Calaf (tenor, gold), Turandot (soprano, ice), Liù (soprano, pink). Ping, Pang and Pong as a comic trio if there's room; the city as a crowd that must not sleep (every cell dimly awake, never resting).

**The toolset test.** A city that can't sleep: the whole grid restless, low, for an act. A riddle: a question as a pattern and its answer as its completion, three times. The moon rising as a dot climbing the far edge. The dawn: the ascension chord move (the purple section) as the grid warms from black to white, one dot at a time.

**Wink candidates.** Ping, Pang and Pong: "PING." "PANG." "PONG." as three dots light in turn. Turandot's "NO!" three times as Calaf answers each riddle.

## What each one adds to the toolset

| Opera | New technique to invent and log |
| --- | --- |
| Dido | Stillness as an act; a ground bass as a repeating visual; a departure by the edge; roses laid, still |
| Barber | Speed and freeze; disguise as colour; slander spreading; a window and a ladder |
| Giovanni | Menace by one-cell steps; a catalogue count; fire rising as a curtain; a moral row |
| Flute | A light show of staccato dots; the ascension ("purple section"); a serpent line; comic stammer |
| Pagliacci | A mask as a colour swap; a stage within the stage; a crowd that laughs at the wrong thing |
| Rigoletto | A curse that returns; a tune from off-grid at the wrong moment; a storm on the whole grid |
| Turandot | A sleepless grid; riddles as pattern and completion; a moonrise; the dawn |
