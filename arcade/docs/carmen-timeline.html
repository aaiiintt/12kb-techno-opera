Carmen, beat by beat

How the story and the instructions take turns. Nothing here is built yet. Approve it, change it, or answer the questions at the end, and the same grammar goes onto Il barbiere and La traviata.

The grammar

Every moment is one of two kinds, and the screen says which before the brain has to. A WATCH beat is a cutscene: letterbox bars, a story caption, nothing to do. A PLAY beat is a microgame: the fuse bar, a verb, one input icon, and it only appears when there is something to do. The player never has to guess.

WATCH
JOSÉ IS HER GUARD.
  • Letterbox bars top and bottom. No fuse. No verb.
  • One caption, present tense, typed in on the beat. It says what is happening and who feels what.
  • Characters move on their own. The music carries on.
  • Four to six seconds. A tap skips nothing on the first play (question 3).
PLAY
UNTIE!
TAP TAP TAP
  • The verb card snaps in with its input icon under it: UNTIE!
  • The fuse burns along the top for exactly the play window, then stops.
  • The icon stays beside the character until the first correct input, then fades.
  • Win or lose lands in the first frame after, and the story is the same either way.

Four inputs, four pictograms

Only these four, so a player learns them in the first opera and never again. Touch and mouse are the same gesture. The keyboard is a courtesy, shown small on desktop.

The timeline

Fourteen beats, about 95 seconds. Six to play, seven to watch, one to feel. Times are targets at the act's tempo. Captions are the exact words on screen; character tags sit beside the speaker as now.

    CARMEN title card

    See
    Seville, sand-lit. Carmen in the doorway. The title.
    Hear
    Habanera bass alone, waiting.
    Do
    TAP TO BEGIN The only instruction on the card. Starts the sound.

    Seville WATCH

    See
    The square. The crowd turns as Carmen walks to the factory door. José on guard, not looking. She looks at him.
    Hear

    Caption

    EVERYONE LOOKS AT CARMEN. EXCEPT JOSÉ.
    Story
    Who she is, who he is, and that she has chosen him before he knows it.
  3. 0:088 s

    CATCH! PLAY · drag

    Do
    DRAG TO CATCH THE FLOWER She throws the cassia flower. Slide José under it. Only the flower and José are bright.
    Win
    It lands in his hands. A heart. HE CATCHES IT.
    Lose
    It lands in the dust. He bends and picks it up. HE PICKS IT UP ANYWAY.
    Then
    Both: José holding the flower, Carmen gone inside. HE KEEPS IT. HE IS LOST.
  4. 0:185 s

    The arrest WATCH

    See
    A fight in the factory (a flash, a scream, a knife-shaped shadow). Carmen marched out with her wrists tied. Zuniga hands her to José. Close-up on the rope.
    Hear
    Snare, the Habanera bass slowed, a whistle.
    Caption
    SHE CUTS A GIRL IN A FIGHT. SHE IS ARRESTED.
    JOSÉ IS HER GUARD.
    Story
    Why she is tied, and why it is his problem.
  5. 0:257 s

    UNTIE! PLAY · tap tap tap

    Do
    TAP TAP TAP TO UNTIE HER Close on the wrists. Each tap loosens a coil; the coils are the meter, no bar.
    Win
    The rope bursts. She's off.
    Lose
    She wriggles free of the last coil herself, pushes him over, and runs.
    Then
    Both: José on the ground, rope on the floor, Zuniga in the doorway. SHE RUNS. JOSÉ GOES TO JAIL FOR HER.
  6. 0:345 s

    The tavern WATCH

    See
    Lillas Pastia's. Dim, warm. Carmen dancing with her back to the door. José walks in with the flower, dried and grey.
    Hear
    The Habanera in the major, soft, a guitar arpeggio.
    Caption
    TWO MONTHS LATER. OUT OF JAIL.
    HE KEPT THE FLOWER THE WHOLE TIME.
    Story
    Time has passed; the flower is now a relic; she has not been waiting.
  7. 0:418 s

    HOLD! PLAY · hold

    Do
    HOLD TO MAKE IT BLOOM José kneels and holds out the flower. While held it blooms red; let go and it wilts. A hold ring fills round the icon.
    Win
    Full bloom. She turns. Petals.
    Lose
    It droops. She turns anyway and laughs.
    Then

  8. 0:516 s

    Escamillo WATCH

    See
    The door bangs open. Escamillo in gold. The tavern cheers. Carmen looks at him. Cut to a close-up of José: he turns green from the feet up.
    Hear

    Caption
    ESCAMILLO. THE TOREADOR.
    JOSÉ IS JEALOUS. GREEN = JEALOUS
    Story
    The rival, and the colour rule spelled out once so it reads for the rest of the opera.
  9. 0:598 s

    FLIP! PLAY · tap

    Do
    TAP TO TURN ALL 3 CARDS The smugglers' camp at night. Carmen reads her fortune. The next card glows; tap when it glows. Three cards, three taps, three spades.
    Win
    All three turned in time. She reads them twice.
    Lose
    The wind turns the rest. Same cards.
    Then
    Both: three spades on the table. THE CARDS SAY DEATH. SHE SHRUGS.
  10. 1:094 s

    The bullring WATCH

    See

    Hear

    Caption
    SEVILLE. THE BULLRING. CARMEN IS WITH ESCAMILLO NOW.
    Story

  11. 1:158 s

    GLORY! PLAY · tap

    Do
    TAP TO JUMP THE BULL You are Escamillo. Three charges, alternating sides. Tap as the bull reaches you.
    Win
    Three clean passes, OLÉ each time, roses.
    Lose
    Tossed, lands, gets up, bows anyway.
    Then
    Both: Escamillo saluting, the crowd on its feet, José's shadow closer. THE CROWD IS HIS. JOSÉ WAITS OUTSIDE.
  12. 1:256 s

    Outside the arena WATCH

    See
    Empty square, cheers from inside. José walks to Carmen, a step on every beat. She does not move. He holds out her ring.
    Hear
    Ring-modulated tenor on the beat, the crowd far away.
    Caption
    JOSÉ BEGS HER TO COME BACK.
    JAMAIS ! beside Carmen.
    Story
    The walk is watched, not played: it is his act, not the player's. This is the beat you said felt like accidentally dabbing her.
  13. 1:336 s

    REJECT! PLAY · tap

    Do
    TAP TO THROW THE RING BACK The ring glows in her hand when he is close enough. One tap in the glow. Early taps: NOT YET.
    Win
    She throws it in his face.
    Lose
    She lets it drop at his feet.
    Then
    Both: the ring on the ground between them. Silence. The knife. White flash. She falls. Two beats later, beside José: MERDE !

What this changes in the build

  • 1The runner gets a third kind of beat, WATCH, with letterbox bars, a typed caption and no fuse. The curtain cards go: the WATCH beats do their job with story instead of a title. The act counter stays tiny in the corner.
  • 2The command card carries the verb, the full instruction line and the input icon: CATCH! big, DRAG TO CATCH THE FLOWER under it, the icon animating. The icon then sits beside the character until the first correct input.
  • 3The four icons live in the runner and every opera uses only those four. Counts go in the words when they matter (ALL 3 CARDS).
  • 4Touch and mouse become direct: tap and drag anywhere on the stage. The on-screen gamepad goes (question 2).
  • 5Carmen's Act I becomes CATCH again; the petals move to the end as the lament. The toy at the end has no fuse and no verb, only the one small icon.
  • 6Plot points get their own WATCH beats and close-ups: the arrest, the two months, Escamillo's entrance with JOSÉ IS JEALOUS, the bullring.

Questions for you

  1. Length. With seven WATCH beats and the petals, Carmen runs about 95 seconds, not "about a minute". Keep the story and accept 90 to 100 seconds, or cut the WATCH beats to three seconds each and lose some of the telling?
    My recommendation: accept the length. The story was the thing you said was missing.
  2. Input. Drop the on-screen gamepad and make touch and mouse direct (tap anywhere, drag anywhere), with the keyboard as a courtesy for desktop?
    My recommendation: yes. Four gestures, one grammar, no controller chrome on the stage.
  3. Skipping. Can a tap during a WATCH beat skip it? On a first play I'd say no (the story is the point); on a replay after the result card, yes.
    My recommendation: skippable only on replays.
  4. The finale. Keep the ring as the last PLAY (her defiance is hers to do), with the knife always following? Or make the whole ending a WATCH beat and let the cards be the last thing the player does?
    My recommendation: keep the ring as a play. Losing it (letting it drop) is still hers.
  5. Narration voice. Present tense, short, a little dry: HE KEEPS IT. HE IS LOST. / SHE SHRUGS. Is that the register, or warmer and more operatic?
    My recommendation: this register. It is the wink's register, so the wink doesn't jump out of nowhere.
  6. The petals. Purely watched, or does a touch stir them and sound a note? And should José walk in them (drag) or stand still and glow?
    My recommendation: he stands and glows; a touch stirs the petals and plays a note. Playful, but the scene stays hers.
  7. Score. Six bravos or a simpler "you saw the whole opera" card with no count, now that the story is doing more of the work?
    My recommendation: keep the count. It is the reason to play twice.
  8. Order of work. Carmen first end to end on this grammar, then the same runner changes flow into the other two with their own timelines for approval?
    My recommendation: yes, and the runner changes (WATCH beats, icons, direct touch) are shared, so the other two are mostly writing.
