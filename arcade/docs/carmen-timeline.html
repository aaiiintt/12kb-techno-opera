Carmen, beat by beat

How the story and the instructions take turns. Revised after Iain's notes: the ring is watched, not played; the knife is spelled out beat by beat; the decisions are logged at the end. This is what gets built, then the same grammar goes onto Il barbiere and La traviata.

The grammar

Every moment is one of two kinds, and the screen says which before the brain has to. A WATCH beat is a cutscene: letterbox bars, a story caption, nothing to do. A PLAY beat is a microgame: the fuse bar, a verb, one input icon, and it only appears when there is something to do. The player never has to guess.

WATCH
JOSÉ IS HER GUARD.
  • Letterbox bars top and bottom. No fuse. No verb.
  • One caption, present tense, typed in on the beat. It says what is happening and who feels what.
  • Characters move on their own. The music carries on.
  • Four to six seconds. A tap skips nothing on the first play (question 3).
PLAY
UNTIE!
TAP TAP TAP
  • The verb card snaps in with its input icon under it: UNTIE!
  • The fuse burns along the top for exactly the play window, then stops.
  • The icon stays beside the character until the first correct input, then fades.
  • Win or lose lands in the first frame after, and the story is the same either way.

Four inputs, four pictograms

Only these four, so a player learns them in the first opera and never again. Touch and mouse are the same gesture. The keyboard is a courtesy, shown small on desktop.

The timeline

Fifteen beats, about 1:55, under the two-minute ceiling. Five to play, eight to watch, one to feel. Times are targets at the act's tempo. Captions are the exact words on screen; character tags sit beside the speaker as now.

    Hear

    CATCH! PLAY · drag

    Do
    DRAG TO CATCH THE FLOWER She throws the cassia flower. Slide José under it. Only the flower and José are bright.

    Lose

    UNTIE! PLAY · tap tap tap

    Do
    TAP TAP TAP TO UNTIE HER Close on the wrists. Each tap loosens a coil; the coils are the meter, no bar.
    Win
    The rope bursts. She's off.
    Lose
    She wriggles free of the last coil herself, pushes him over, and runs.
    Then
    Both: José on the ground, rope on the floor, Zuniga in the doorway. SHE RUNS. JOSÉ GOES TO JAIL FOR HER.

    JOSÉ IS JEALOUS. GREEN = JEALOUS

    All three turned in time. She reads them twice.
    Lose
    The wind turns the rest. Same cards.
    Then
    Both: three spades on the table. THE CARDS SAY DEATH. SHE SHRUGS.

  11. 1:158 s

    GLORY! PLAY · tap

    Do
    TAP TO JUMP THE BULL You are Escamillo. Three charges, alternating sides. Tap as the bull reaches you.
    Win

    Lose

    Outside the arena WATCH

    See
    Empty square, cheers from inside the ring. Carmen alone by the gate in red. José walks in from the left, a step on every beat, green-rimmed. She does not move. He kneels and holds out her ring.
    Hear

    Caption
    JOSÉ BEGS HER TO COME BACK.
    JAMAIS ! beside Carmen (never).
    Story

  13. 1:338 s

    The knife WATCH

    See
    Second by second, the whole thing on one screen, mid shot:
    1. 0 s: She pulls the ring off and throws it at his feet. It bounces once. SHE THROWS HIS RING BACK.
    2. 2 s: Close on José. The green drains to black. A knife appears in his hand, the brightest thing on screen. JOSÉ HAS A KNIFE.
    3. 4 s: One step. White flash, one frame. Screen shakes. She folds to the ground, red on sand. HE STABS HER.
    4. 6 s: Inside the arena the crowd roars OLÉ ! for Escamillo. Nobody sees. INSIDE, THE CROWD CHEERS ESCAMILLO.
    5. 7 s: Beside Carmen on the ground, small: MERDE... The wink: her last word, deadpan.
    Hear
    The fate motif once, the OLÉ from far off, then silence for the wink.
    Story
    Four captions, each one a plain fact, so nobody has to guess what the flash was. No play here: it is not the player's to do or to fail.
  14. 1:4312 s

    Libre TOY · no fuse, no verb

    See
    The stage empties to black. José alone, glowing faintly. Red petals begin to fall, slowly, everywhere. This is the poetic scene from the Dot Opera's last act, moved here where it belongs.
    Hear

    Do
    Nothing required. A touch or a drag anywhere stirs the petals and sounds a soft note. A tiny TAP icon appears once and fades on first touch.
    Caption
    SHE IS FREE. Nothing else.

What this changes in the build

  • 1The runner gets a third kind of beat, WATCH, with letterbox bars, a typed caption and no fuse. The curtain cards go: the WATCH beats do their job with story instead of a title. The act counter stays tiny in the corner.
  • 2The command card carries the verb, the full instruction line and the input icon: CATCH! big, DRAG TO CATCH THE FLOWER under it, the icon animating. The icon then sits beside the character until the first correct input.
  • 3The four icons live in the runner and every opera uses only those four. Counts go in the words when they matter (ALL 3 CARDS).
  • 4Touch and mouse become direct: tap and drag anywhere on the stage. The on-screen gamepad goes.
  • 5Carmen's Act I becomes CATCH again; the petals move to the end as the lament. The toy at the end has no fuse and no verb, only the one small icon.
  • 6Plot points get their own WATCH beats and close-ups: the arrest, the two months, Escamillo's entrance with JOSÉ IS JEALOUS, the bullring, and the knife spelled out in four captions.
  • 7GLORY, jumping the bull, is the last thing the player does. The ending after it is all watched.

Decisions (Iain, 29 Sep)

  1. Length: the story and the interactions set it; the ceiling is two minutes. Carmen sits at about 1:55.
  2. Input: direct touch and mouse, tap and drag anywhere. The on-screen gamepad goes; the keyboard stays for desktop.
  3. No skipping WATCH beats. They are short.
  4. The ring is not played. Jumping the bull is the last play; the ring, the knife and the petals are watched.
  5. Narration stays short, dry, present tense. Every word counts.
  6. The petals: José stands and glows; a touch stirs them and sounds a note.
  7. Bravos stay, now out of five.
  8. Carmen first, end to end. Then the skill and the notes are updated and Il barbiere and La traviata get their timelines on the same runner.
